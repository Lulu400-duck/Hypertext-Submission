  • Gaspara Stampa

    A Venetian poet considered one of the greatest Italian lyric poets of the Renaissance. She wrote over 300 sonnets inspired by her unrequited love for Count Collaltino di Collalto, published posthumously as Rime (1554). Her work is celebrated for its emotional intensity and female perspective in a male-dominated literary world.

    "Se Voi volete conoscere il mio signore, di bel e dolce aspetto cercate"

  • Arcangela Tarabotti

    A Venetian nun and fierce polemicist who wrote radical critiques of the forced monachization of women in 17th-century Venice. Despite being confined to a convent against her will, she produced bold works like Paternal Tyranny and Women Are No Less Rational Than Men, making her one of the earliest feminist voices in Italian literature.

    "Non mi burlate se io con penna di candida colomba, funesto corvo vi auguro nel vostro Inferno"

  • Lucrezia Marinella

    A prolific Venetian writer and one of the most important female intellectuals of the late Renaissance. Her landmark work The Nobility and Excellence of Women (1600) directly challenged misogynist writings of the time, arguing systematically for the moral and intellectual superiority of women. She also wrote poetry, epics, and religious works.

    "È adunque primiera, e principal cagione la belleza divina della beltá donnesca, doppo la quale vi concorrono le stelle, il cielo, la natura, e gli elementi."
